# BYOD & SETA Policy Framework for Codbase

## 📌 Project Overview
This project involved conducting a comprehensive risk analysis of Bring Your Own Device (BYOD) threats and evaluating the existing Security Education, Training, and Awareness (SETA) program for **Codbase**, a small IT firm with fewer than ten staff. 

The goal was to interview the company's IT lead (Mr. Paul) and develop a future-ready BYOD policy framework that could be activated when the company is ready, taking into account their cost constraints and existing infrastructure.

## Key Technical Areas Covered
Through this project, I gained hands-on experience in translating enterprise security concepts into actionable policies:
*   **Technical Controls:** Aligning policy with existing controls like ACLs, VPN, MFA and Active Directory (AD).
*   **Data Protection:** Structuring sections on acceptable use, data protection and incident management (including MDM, MAM, and remote wipe concepts).
*   **Compliance:** Ensuring the policy respected employee privacy and aligned with the **Malaysia PDPA** (Personal Data Protection Act).

## Project Reflections

**What I Loved:**
Working on the BYOD policy felt like solving a real problem for a real company. Mr. Paul was honest about the cost and fairness concerns of BYOD. Because the company wasn't ready to adopt it yet, I enjoyed structuring the policy as a framework they could activate later—matching sections on secure access and data protection to Codbase's existing controls.

**What I Found Challenging (Loathed):**
Initially, I felt my technical knowledge was not strong enough. Mr. Paul discussed terms like AD, Microsoft Intune, PAM and session tokens. I had to independently research several of these terms before I could write about them properly. It taught me the importance of researching deeply and checking my work against the interview transcript early on.

**What I Learned:**
I learned that a BYOD policy is much more than just letting staff use their phones. It is about deciding what data can go on a personal device, separating work/personal use, handling lost devices and protecting privacy under the PDPA. I learned how to read an interview transcript and turn it into technical policy sections and how to explain complex controls like MDM and MFA in plain language. Most importantly, I learned that a good policy must respect the company's actual financial situation.

**What I Longed For (Next Steps):**
I wished for more time to research affordable MDM vs. MAM options, as cost was Mr. Paul's biggest concern. If given more time, I would provide a clearer cost-recommendation matrix and conduct a full team review to catch small inconsistencies before the final submission.
